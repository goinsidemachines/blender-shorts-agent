# 07 Checks

A short must never go out with wrong animation, missing textures or render artifacts. Two kinds of checks catch
them: code checks, and **your own eyes**.

## Run the code checks

`BLENDER check SHORT` runs every scene check over every shot (every 5th frame plus each shot's last; pops use every
frame, and anything that moves or deforms is checked for overlaps on every frame, so a part that passes through
another between two samples is still found) and writes `review/checks.json`, errors first. It marks the `checks` stage **only when there are no error
findings**; with errors it exits non-zero. Warnings do not block, but read each one and fix it unless it is clearly
intended (say why in your notes).

Fix, rebuild (`06-build.md`), check again, after every fix: a fix moves parts, and moved parts make new
artifacts. `checks.json` is stamped with the build and the timing: the final render
and edit refuse checks that are missing, failed, or ran on an older build or timing, so check again after every
rebuild. A look at the work may check with a coarse `--every` (`--every 20`), but the final render and edit need
the default 5 or finer (so a `preview --every 10` chain runs its check step each time rather than skip it: the
shots it already checked at that `--every` are reused, so it is quick). A preview rendered while the checks found errors on the build as it is still renders, but
warns with their count and the first few (in its result and render status): read it, as the preview shows them and
the final render refuses until they are fixed and checked again. If an error is still there after 3 real attempts, stop and tell the user
what fails, what you tried, and the options (for example: simplify the shot, accept a badge, change the camera).

### Cutaway models

A cutaway (section) model meets three checks. `normals` (non-manifold hero) warns on the open cut edges until the
section face closes them; add the face rather than silencing the warning. `overlap` should report only where parts
really meet: the section face lies on the cut plane, so keep it clear of the solids it caps (0.5 to 1 mm). `volume`
gas is left out of `overlap`, so cut it at the same plane yourself (`06-build.md`, "Cutaway (section) models").

## Every check, and how to fix the usual causes

Scene checks (`bsa/blender/checks.py`, in `review/checks.json`):

| Check | Severity | What it means | Usual causes and fixes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `images` | error | An image texture node (or the world's environment texture) has no image, or its file is missing: renders pink. | A path to a file that was moved or never downloaded; an empty Image Texture node left in. Load from the path `assets download` returned, use `look.image_material` (it packs images), delete unused nodes. |
| `materials` | error | Geometry in view (a mesh, curve, text label, surface or metaball, or an instanced object) has no material: renders default grey. | Forgot `look.assign`; a modifier or join created a new object; an imported model lost its slots; a text label made without a material. Assign a real material to everything visible. |
| `instances` | warning | Instanced geometry (a collection instance, geometry-node instances) is checked for materials and images only: overlaps, normals, grounding and pops are not checked on it. | Append the model's objects instead of instancing a collection (`assets.md`), or check those instances yourself in the stills and frames. |
| `normals` (flipped) | error | Some faces are wound the opposite way to their neighbours: dark patches, wrong shading, broken bevels. | Mirrored or joined geometry, hand-built faces in the wrong order. Recalculate normals outside (`bmesh.ops.recalc_face_normals`) in build.py. |
| `normals` (inside out) | error | A closed shell has negative volume: the whole object is inside out. | Negative scale applied, faces built clockwise. Recalculate normals, or apply scale before building faces. |
| `normals` (non-manifold hero) | warning | The hero mesh has open edges. | Missing caps, duplicate vertices. Merge by distance, fill holes; open edges show as gaps and break bevels. |
| `faceted` | warning | A curved surface has many edges between 1° and 25° shaded flat: it looks faceted. | Flat shading on a cylinder or sphere. `look.smooth(obj)` (keeps sharp edges sharp), more segments, or a bevel modifier with segments. `look.smooth` then `look.bevel` (hardened normals) renders smooth and is not reported, on boxes and on cylinders, cones and rings alike. |
| `overlap` (z-fighting) | error | Two objects have coplanar faces facing the same way, overlapping: they flicker in the render. How close counts depends on how far the shot camera is: at most 0.1 mm (far away), down to 0.01 mm close up, so a small part a hair inside another near the camera is fine. Faces exactly on one plane always count. The finding gives both objects' sizes, one place where the faces overlap (world x, y, z) and how far apart they are. | A plate laid exactly on a floor, two copies at the same spot, a decal with no offset. Move one by 0.5 to 1 mm or delete the duplicate face. |
| `overlap` (interpenetrate) | error | Two objects pass through each other. The finding names the frames and how deep the deeper one goes (`'Rod' is 4 cm inside 'Block'`). When the other mesh is not closed (holes, open parts) and not a flat sheet like a floor, a depth would mean nothing: the finding says it is not closed, with its count of open edges. A pair that overlaps the same way in every frame (scenery that never moves) says "in every frame" and is listed once with all its shots. The first 20 pairs are listed (a pair counts once, in any number of shots); one more finding says how many pairs were not. | Wrong motion (a part swings into another), wrong sizes, a clearance that is too small, a linkage built from an axis instead of from a surface. Fix the motion or the geometry. Tag `contact:<other>` only for real surface contact (below). Siblings under one parent or assembly empty are checked like any other pair. Not tested: a pair inside one `rigid` group (one solid built from several meshes: a parent and its children that don't move on their own), a pair of members of one `assembly:<name>` (with `assembly:<name>:<max depth m>` they are tested against that depth), and any pair with a `volume` (gas, flame). A member of either group against anything outside it is checked as usual. Several meshes that are one solid: join them or tag the parent `rigid` (`06-build.md`). |
| `overlap` (joined / interpenetrating parts excused by contact tags) | error | A pair tagged `contact:<other>`, or a direct parent and child, overlaps deeper than it may: by default the smaller of 5 cm and 10% of the thinner part's smallest dimension (at least 2 mm), so a 1 cm rod may sink 2 mm. The finding names the pair, the depth, the frames and the allowance. | A rod that runs to the centre of the hub, ball or bob it is attached to; a cable whose length was set by eye and ends inside what it holds; a sliding part pushed into a solid body. End each part at the other's surface, computed from the geometry; make bodies that something slides into hollow. For the rare part that is deep by design (a pin seated in a board), tag `contact:<other>:<max depth in metres>`, e.g. `contact:Board:0.015`, and write why next to the tag. Only a direct parent/child pair is excused (and depth-limited the same way); a grandparent, or two children of one parent, are not. |
| `self_zfight` | error (different materials), warning (one material) | Faces of one mesh lie on the same plane (within 0.1 mm, less when the camera is close), face the same way and overlap: z-fighting inside one object. The finding names both materials, the overlap area and where it is. With two materials it flickers between them; with one it shows as shading noise. Checked on heroes and on every rendered mesh in a shot camera's view. Faces that only share an edge (one flat surface split into quads) are fine, and so are faces on one plane that face opposite ways (where two joined parts meet face to face, inside the solid). | Joined boxes: a ledge or cap flush with the body's top, a column whose outer face lies on the wall's, a window band whose sides lie on the columns', a lug's bottom flush with a ring's bottom. Tuck one face inside the other (make the smaller part 0.5 to 1 mm thinner or sink it into the body), or stop one part at the other's face. |
| `self_overlap` | warning | A hero mesh is made of loose parts (pieces of one mesh not joined by edges) of comparable size that pass into each other deeper than the contact allowance: they render as one solid, so two rods through each other read as one bar. Parts that only touch are fine, and so is detail sunk into a big body (one part with at least 10 times the other's bounding-box volume: ledges set into a building's wall, a pin in a block). A hero whose mesh deforms (shape keys, an armature) is checked at every sampled frame, and there big-body detail warns too when it moves against the body (it sinks in at some frames and not others, or its depth changes). | Two primitives joined into one object without trimming them; a shape key or bone that pushes one part into another. Make them separate objects (then the overlap checks see them), end one part at the other's surface, or fix the deformation. |
| `boolean` | warning | A rendered mesh has a live Boolean modifier. It is evaluated again for every render and can come out differently: an opening that shows in the stills can vanish in the final render (an EXACT cut on a hand-built or non-manifold mesh above all). | A window or cut-away made with a Boolean. Build the opening into the mesh itself (leave out the faces, close the edges), or apply the modifier in build.py and check the face count. |
| `contact_parent` | warning | A pair tagged `contact:<other>` follows different moving parents (one part rides on an animated or constrained parent or grandparent that the other does not), and where they meet drifts apart during a shot. A mesh deformed by a Hook modifier follows its hook objects (and their moving parents), and one with an Armature modifier follows the armature: a tape hooked to empties under its slider follows the slider. The pair warns only when neither part follows the other and they follow different things. All the pair's contact tags count, on either part (one `:moves` among them is enough). The finding names what moves each part. | A part rebuilt with a new constructor that dropped its parent, so it stands still while its partner sways through it. Parent both to the moving part they share. A part that really moves against a fixed one through its parent (a foot stepping on a floor) also warns: then say why in your notes. A part's own animation (a rod sliding in a fixed sleeve) and a hinge on a fixed pin through the meeting point do not warn. When the parts move against each other by design (pendulum balls that strike and swing apart, a pawl on a turning ratchet ring, a tyre on a scrolling floor), tag `contact:<other>:moves` instead, and write why. |
| `floating` | error | An object tagged `grounded` floats above or sinks into what is under it (more than 2 mm), or has nothing under it. A `volume` (gas, flame) is not checked, and never counts as what holds a grounded object up; nor does an `overlay`. | Origin at the centre instead of the base, a support moved, motion lifting it. Compute its Z from the support's top, or bake contact from geometry. |
| `pops` | error | An object jumps in one frame (or two) far more than it moves in the frames on both sides: a velocity discontinuity. Fast motion that then slows or stops (a swing ending in a collision, a struck ball setting off) is not a pop. A shot's first and last frames are judged against the frames inside the short (and, within one scene, the frame just outside the shot): never a frame past the short's end, nor one across a cut to another scene. | A keyframe gap or a missing frame in a baked series, a cut inside one scene, a teleport for a reset, a multi-leg path eased over one parameter (fast on long legs, slow on short ones). Bake every frame (`keyframe_series` step 1), move along paths by arc length (`bake.keyframe_path`), split a cut into two shots, hide the object instead of moving it. If the real motion is this quick (a flick, a snap), tag the part `fast`: the finding says so. |
| `frame_edge` | warning; error when more than half of a hero is outside for half a second or more | A `hero` (or `overlay`) object is partly or wholly outside the camera frame during its shot (a `hero:s07@0.6` hero from 60% of s07 on only). The finding says how much of it is outside at its worst sampled frame ("94% of 'Housing' is outside the camera frame at frame 241") and at which frames. An overlay is only ever a warning. Never for an object tagged `overlay:may-exit`. | Camera too close, the subject moves out, the wrong camera, a camera move keyed only at its two ends (it swings off the subject in between: use `camera.move`, `06-build.md`). Reframe or move the camera; tag only the true subject as hero (`hero:s03,s04` when it is the subject of those shots only, `hero:s07@0.6` when the camera only reaches it 60% into s07). An overlay that leaves the frame by design (a falling stream): tag it `overlay:may-exit`. |
| `pop_in` | warning | An object (not an `overlay`) switches on inside the camera frame after its shot's first frame: its render visibility (`hide_render`, keyed, or a hidden collection) turns on, or it grows from zero scale, while part of its box is on screen. It pops into view. The finding names the object, the shot and the frame. A part tagged `swap:<other>` is not a pop-in: the `swap` check looks at it instead. | A part hidden until its moment and switched on where it already is (a key appearing in the lock), a part scaled up from nothing. Start it outside the frame so it moves in from the edge, switch it on at a cut (the shot's first frame) or while something hides it. A part that replaces another in place (a straight blade swapped for a bent one): tag it `swap:<other>`. A deliberate appearance on screen: say why in your notes. |
| `swap` | warning | A part tagged `swap:<other>` is switched on inside its shot (after the first frame) to take over from `<other>`, but `<other>` is not switched off within a frame of it (the off frame nearest to it, or never in the shot), or their boxes on screen (the new part's at its first frame, the old one's at its last) differ by more than 2% of the frame's width or height. Both show at once, the view blinks empty, or the part jumps. The finding names both parts, the shot, the frame and what is wrong. | The new part keyed on a frame after the old one goes off, or built at a slightly different place or size. Put the new part exactly where the old one was and switch both on the same frame (the new one on, the old one off). |
| `camera_below_ground` | error | The shot camera is under the top surface of an object that acts as ground: a large, roughly flat, horizontal mesh (at least 1 m and the subject's width each way, thin for its size) under the shot's heroes (or, with none, under everything else). The shot sees the ground's underside: a grey slab drifting through the frame. The finding names the ground, the frames, where the camera is and how far below the top. | An automatic framing (`frame_objects`) that centred a tall subject along its height and put the camera under the floor, a ground made bigger after the camera was placed, a camera move that dips below the floor. Place the camera above the ground's top (frame the subject from above its base into the default band, `bsa.layout.target_area`, which `camera.frame_objects` / `frame_box` use), or lower the ground. A shot meant to look from below ground needs the ground hidden there. |
| `covered` | warning; error over 50% for half a second or more | A caption, the badge, a stacked label or a near label covers more than 25% of a hero's box on screen (or of the object a near label names: a label over its own part counts), at the sampled frames. The boxes are the ones the edit draws (`bsa.overlays`, the same placement as the edit, without rendering): the caption band at the bottom, the badge and stacked labels in the top-left corner, near labels just above their object. With a brand kit the boxes are the kit's cards, as the edit places them: title card, value bars, category and step tags, logo badge, stacked and near labels (with their colour dots) and the caption bar. It does not measure the progress bar, nor a near label's pointer line and the dot at its anchor end (thin, and the dot sits on the part on purpose). | The subject framed into the caption band or under the badge corner, a long label, a near label pushed onto its part because there is no room above it (the part at the top of the frame). Frame the subject in the default band, `bsa.layout.target_area` (above the caption band, below the top cards; `camera.frame_objects` / `frame_box` frame into it), shorten the text, or move the camera so the part has room above it. |
| `framing` | warning | For most of a shot (60% of its sampled frames), the heroes' box on screen covers under 2% of the frame (the subject is small), or one half (top or bottom) holds no hero at all (the subject sits in one half, the other is empty). Any part on screen that is not scenery or an overlay fills the top half too (the machine round a small hero), and a top-half warning names the largest parts on screen, to tag the true subject `hero`. The halves split at the centre of the default framing band (`bsa.layout.target_area`, about row 731), not the middle of the frame, so a subject `camera.frame_objects` / `frame_box` centred in that band, however wide, is not reported. A small subject is only reported as small. Shots without heroes are not checked. | A camera too far away, a framing centred on the whole safe area so the subject sits in the top third over an empty floor, a pull-back that ends wide. Move the camera in or use a longer lens; `camera.frame_objects` / `frame_box` frame the subject between the top cards and the caption band by default (`06-build.md`). A deliberate wide or establishing shot (or a deliberate empty half): give the shot `"wide": true`, the reason (`"wide": "the whole bridge in its valley"`), or `[[from_s, to_s], ...]` (seconds from the shot's start, like `hold`) in short.json. |
| `names` (renamed duplicate) | warning | An object name ends in `.001`, `.002`...: Blender renamed it because the name was taken. Object names are shared by every scene in the .blend, so a label or tag using the plain name points at the other scene's object. One warning names every renamed object. | Two scenes each making `Person`, or a rebuild that did not remove the old objects. Give each object a name unique across the short (`Person (lab)`, `Person (street)`), or remove the old scenes before building. |
| `names` (missing name) | error | A `near` label in short.json names an object its shot's scene does not have, a `contact:` or `swap:` tag names an object the scene does not have, or a `hero:` tag names a shot short.json does not have. A `hero:` shot whose `@` fraction is not a number between 0 and 1 (`hero:s07@1.2`) is an error too. A tag the checks do not know (a typo such as `contcat:Floor`) or one with nothing after its prefix (`contact:`, `category:`, `hero:`, `swap:`, `assembly:`) is an error too, with the nearest known tag (`bake.tag` refuses both). The finding suggests the `.001` name when there is one. | A typo, a renamed duplicate, an object built in another scene. Use the object's exact name and a tag from the table below, and rename duplicates. |
| `hard_rule` | warning | An object, mesh, material, collection or linked library name has a word one of the user's hard rules may forbid (`no_humans`, `no_animals`, `no_faces`, `no_brand_logos`): a "Worker" object, a "Skin" material. A collection is reported once, as itself. Names Blender gives its own data (`RigidBodyWorld`, `RigidBodyConstraints`) and the phrases "rigid body" and "soft body" are not flagged. Only with such a rule. | A downloaded model or material that shows what a rule forbids: remove it. An innocent name (a clock's "HourHand"): rename it, or clear the word (or the phrase it is innocent in, `"logo eye"`) with its reason in `short.json` `rules_check` (`02-story.md`). Never leave one unanswered. |
| `set_clearance` | warning | A prop of the brand kit's set (tagged `set:prop`) meets the subject on screen at a sampled frame, or a card, caption or label of the edit, or a strip the Shorts player covers, by any overlap at all. It compares the prop's screen box with the shot's heroes' boxes (with no hero, every other rendered mesh that is not scenery or an overlay), with the kit's cards as the edit draws them, and with those strips: the rule `sets.dress` placed it by. One warning per prop and shot, naming the frames. | `sets.dress` reuses the approved arrangement as it is, so a new timing, camera or motion can put a kept prop in the way. Ask the user, then place it again with `sets.dress(sc, kit, short, tl_obj, short_dir, replace=["toolbox"])` (the prop's name as the finding gives it), rebuild and check again. Never delete the prop to silence it, and do not tag it `contact:`. |
| `framing_call` | warning | build.py's `camera.frame_objects` / `frame_box` gave an answer that cannot be right: the camera sent far off, the subject outside the view or past the clip end, the fit not settling, or (with `above_ground=False` only) the camera under the ground. The warnings are kept on the scene when the build frames, per camera and key frame, and listed for the shot they were framed for (`shot=`) or whose frames hold the key. The preview chain's build step names them too. | Read the warning, fix the framing call (the camera's start angle, the objects, `aim=True`, the clip end), rebuild and look at the still. Framing the same camera and key again without a problem clears it. |

Tags the checks read (`bake.tag(obj, ...)` in build.py, `06-build.md`):

| Tag | What it does |
|---|---|
| `hero` / `hero:s03,s04` / `hero:s07@0.6` | The subject (of those shots only): `frame_edge` warns when it leaves the frame; `self_overlap` and `normals` (non-manifold) look at it; `covered`, `framing` and `camera_below_ground` measure it on screen; the `shading` frame check looks at its large flat faces. `hero:s07@0.6` is a hero of s07 from 60% of the shot on (a subject the camera reaches later): `frame_edge` and `framing` ignore it before; `covered`, `camera_below_ground` and the rest see it for the whole shot. The fraction is a number between 0 and 1 (`hero:s07@0.6,s08` mixes both). |
| `grounded` | Rests on something: `floating` errors when it floats or sinks more than 2 mm. |
| `contact:<other>` / `contact:<other>:<max depth m>` | Really touches `<other>`: the pair may overlap up to the contact allowance (or the stated depth); `contact_parent` watches the pair's parents. |
| `contact:<other>:moves` / `contact:<other>:<max depth m>:moves` | Touches `<other>` at some point and moves against it by design (parts that strike and part, slide, roll or hinge). The depth limit holds as for `contact:`; `contact_parent` does not warn about the pair. Depth and `moves` combine: a tape tooth that slides into the slider and may sink 4 mm: `contact:Slider:0.004:moves`. When a pair has several contact tags (on either part), they merge: the deepest stated depth, and `moves` if any says so. |
| `overlay` | Not physical geometry: a glow halo, a distance or guide line, an arrow. Left out of `overlap` and z-fighting (as either object of a pair), `self_zfight`, `self_overlap`, `floating` (and never counted as what holds a grounded object up) and `pops`. Still checked for `materials`, `images`, `normals` and `faceted`, and kept inside the frame like a hero (`frame_edge`). Only for things that are drawn on the scene, never to silence a real part. |
| `overlay:may-exit` | An `overlay` (left out of the same checks) that leaves the frame on purpose: water falling out of shot, a stream running down a pipe past the frame's edge. `frame_edge` does not warn about it (not even when it is also a `hero`). Only for motion that is meant to leave the frame, never to hide a badly framed subject. |
| `surface` | An open fluid surface that things may cross: water moved by waves (a grid with an Ocean modifier), a liquid level. Floating hulls and a wheel dipping into it are not overlaps. Left out of `overlap` and z-fighting (as either object of a pair) while its mesh is open; every other check still runs, and it still holds up a grounded object. A closed mesh is a solid and is checked whatever its tags: a `surface` tag on one is a `names` warning. Use `overlay` only for what is not physical at all. |
| `category:<name>` | Not a check of its own: the part's category in the brand kit (`brand.categories`, lower case). A part label near it shows a dot in that category's colour, and the `covered` check measures that label with its dot. Without a brand kit it changes nothing. |
| `set:floor` / `set:wall` / `set:backdrop` / `set:prop` | Scenery a brand kit's set made (its floor, wall, backdrop, or a prop placed round the subject). Never a hero (`bake.tag` refuses both on one object). The ink lines leave out a set's floor, wall and backdrop. |
| `lines:off` | Not a check of its own: the ink lines (Freestyle) are not drawn round this part. `overlay` parts get no lines either. |
| `swap:<other>` | Takes over from `<other>` on screen in place (a straight blade swapped for a bent one): no `pop_in` warning when it switches on; `swap` checks that `<other>` goes off on the same frame and that the new part is where it was. A `swap:` tag naming an object the scene does not have is a `names` error. |
| `fast` | May move more than the pop limit in one frame; still checked for overlaps, `frame_edge` and `pop_in`. Only for a part whose real motion is that quick (a flick, a snap), never to hide a keyframe gap. |
| `rigid` | A forged part built from several meshes (a crank and its pin): a parent and its children that don't move on their own. It and every object under it (children, grandchildren) is one solid, so `overlap` does not test them against each other; a child with its own keys, drivers, NLA strips or constraints, and everything under it, is not in the group (tag it `rigid` too to start a group of its own). Each is still checked against everything outside the group. Joining the meshes into one is simpler. |
| `assembly:<name>` / `assembly:<name>:<max depth m>` | Members of one assembly (a gear train, a balance and its arbor) may touch or mesh with each other only: `overlap` does not test a pair of members, or tests it against the stated depth (the deeper one either member states; the deeper of it and any `contact:` tag or parent/child allowance on the pair). A member against a non-member (a balance wheel against its cock) is checked as usual. `assembly:Gear:train` is the assembly "Gear:train": the last part is a depth only when it is a number. |
| `volume` | Gas, flame or smoke round solids: left out of `overlap` and z-fighting (as either object of a pair), `self_zfight`, `self_overlap` and `floating` (and never counted as what holds a grounded object up). Still checked for `pops`, `pop_in`, `frame_edge` (when a hero), `materials` and `images`, and seen by every on-screen measure: it is scene, not an overlay. |

Set objects (the floor, walls, backdrop and props of a kit's set) are scenery. They are never heroes (they are not
framed, and `frame_edge`, `self_overlap` and the `covered` subjects skip them), and two set objects are never tested
against each other. A set object against any other part is still tested, so a prop sunk into the subject is found. The
style's ink lines are Freestyle and add no geometry, so they change no check, and a style's bevel modifiers are
switched off while the checks measure (and put back after): the checks see the parts as modelled, in every style.

At most 20 overlapping pairs are listed as findings; one more finding says how many pairs were left out. Every pair,
listed or not, is still in one check run: `overlap_summary` (in review/checks.json and the check's result) groups them
by name family, real clips first, deepest first, then z-fighting, such as `Tape left vs Tooth left 01-22: 22 pairs, up
to 1 cm, s01-s05`. Read it to fix a whole family at once (one cause, such as a tape laid through every tooth), then
check again.

### Contact tags: only for real contact

- **`contact:` only for real surface contact:** a foot on a floor, a ball in its cradle, a cable at its anchor.
  Never to silence an overlap you have not looked at. If a pair needs `contact:`, write why next to the tag in
  build.py (`bake.tag(cable, "contact:Ball")  # the cable ends on the ball's surface at its anchor`). The tag
  allows a touch, not a part buried in the other: the checks still report it, with its depth.
- **Run an unfiltered intersection pass before the preview** and look at every pair it lists: no contact or parent
  exemptions, every ~10 frames plus each shot's last frame. Most pairs are intended touches; the ones that are not
  are artifacts. In Blender (the MCP, or a background Blender on blend/short.blend), for each shot's scene and
  frame: build a `mathutils.bvhtree.BVHTree.FromPolygons` from each rendered mesh's evaluated, world-space vertices
  and polygons, and list every pair whose trees `overlap()`. BVH overlap also reports pure touching, so read each
  pair: depth is what separates a touch from an artifact, and the full-size frames at the extremes of the motion
  show it.

Frame checks (`bsa/image_checks.py`): `BLENDER frame-check SHORT --preset preview|final` checks the rendered PNGs and
writes `review/frame-checks-<preset>.json`; it exits non-zero on errors.

| Check | Severity | What it means | Usual causes and fixes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unreadable_frame` | error | The PNG is not complete (empty, cut off or zero-filled): it is never decoded or checked further. | A render interrupted by a crash or power cut. Render it again (the finding's `redo`, or `--redo-flagged`); a resumed `render` also finds and redoes these itself. |
| `black_frame` | error | A frame's mean brightness is near zero. | No light in the scene, the camera inside an object, a world strength of 0, the wrong camera. Light it, move the camera. |
| `fireflies` | warning over 25, error over 200 | Isolated very bright pixels (noise). | Too few samples, caustics from glass or metal, tiny strong lights. Cycles: keep denoising on, raise samples, clamp indirect light (`scene.cycles.sample_clamp_indirect = 10`), turn off caustics, use larger lights. |
| `stuck_frame` | warning | A frame is identical to the one before it in the middle of motion: a stutter. Reported only where a hero of the shot (or, with no hero tagged, any object) or the camera is keyed to a different place than at the frame before, so a part at rest or a scripted freeze is not flagged; never inside the shot's `hold`. | A missing key, a frame rendered twice, motion on twos. Bake every frame; re-render the shot (the finding's `redo`). A deliberate freeze the heroes' keys don't show (an effect paused, say): give the shot `"hold": true` or `"hold": [[from_s, to_s], ...]` (seconds from the shot's start) in short.json. |
| `flicker` | warning | An area changes for one frame and comes back (A-B-A), bigger than noise: z-fighting or denoiser blotches. Camera moves and pans are not flicker, nor is an object moving: one found again shifted in the frame before or after, or one entering, leaving or crossing the frame (a falling, spinning part): something of the area's size changes for good near it. Many small spots at frames where things move are one finding with their count. | Coplanar faces (see `overlap`), too few samples with denoising. Re-render the frames (the finding's `redo`) after fixing the cause; look at the frame. |
| `shading` | warning | A large flat face of a hero (at least 2% of the frame, seen first, facing the camera) shades strongly and smoothly from one side to the other (its brightness changes by 25% or more of its mean, and by 0.12 at least) in a shot's first, middle or last frame: it looks dirty or curved. A cast shadow's edge is a step, not a gradient, and is not reported. Needs `blend/short.blend` (the faces are found with rays from the shot camera). | Area lights scaled up for a big subject stand too close and fall off across it (a 97 m tower lit from 50 m: its top far brighter than its base). Light subjects larger than a few metres with sun lamps: `look.lights(scene, target, size)` (`look-and-brand.md`). Also check an edge or facing effect in the material (a Layer Weight "Facing" tone varies across a big flat face seen in perspective): limit it to true grazing angles. |

Other checks along the way: `CLI validate SHORT` (unsourced numbers, unknown ids, size and fps), the `length_drift`
from `timeline`, the mix `levels` (`06-build.md`), and `edit`, which probes the MP4 and fails if its size or frame
count is wrong.

## Look at the images yourself (always)

The code cannot see everything. **Read every storyboard still and a sample of preview frames yourself** before
moving on: the stills before the storyboard page, again after the final build, and after the preview render at
least the first, middle and last frame of every shot plus any frame where something important happens (a contact,
a label appearing, a cut) from `frames/preview/<shot>/####.png`. Hunt for:

- **Wrong motion:** parts passing through each other, floating, sliding instead of rolling, moving the wrong way,
  motion out of step with what the line says at that moment.
- **Untextured or pink surfaces:** default grey, missing-texture pink, flat plastic where there should be metal.
- **Flicker and noise:** grain, fireflies, blotchy denoising, z-fighting stripes; compare neighbouring frames for
  flicker.
- **Framing:** subject cut off, labels or the subject under where the platform buttons sit, a horizon that tilts by
  accident.
- **Faceting, harsh black shadows, blown-out highlights.**

Anything wrong is a check failure: fix it the same way (up to 3 attempts, then tell the user).
